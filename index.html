LESSON 01 - ANATOMY & LAYERS

What is an Artificial Neural Network?

An artificial neural network (ANN) organizes compute into distinct layers of neurons (nodes) connected sequentially from input to output:

  • Input Layer: Sensor nodes that accept raw feature vectors (e.g. study hours, sensor readings). Neurons here do not transform data; they distribute inputs forward.
  • Hidden Layer(s): Feature detectors that combine inputs into intermediate mathematical abstractions.
  • Output Layer: Decision nodes generating target predictions (e.g. classification class probabilities).
LESSON 02 - SYNAPSES & WEIGHTS

Synaptic Filaments and Numeric Weights

The white luminous filaments connecting neurons represent synapses. Each synapse carries an adjustable mathematical scalar called a weight (w):

  • Positive weight: Excitatory synapse that amplifies signals passed to the downstream neuron.
  • Negative weight: Inhibitory synapse that dampens or suppresses incoming data.
  • Magnitude (|w|): Governs the strength of the connection. Hover or touch any neuron dot to isolate its incoming fan-in and outgoing fan-out.
LESSON 03 - THE NEURON & RELU

Weighted Summation and Non-Linear Activation

Inside each artificial neuron dot, two fundamental mathematical steps occur before passing output to subsequent layers:

  • Weighted Sum: Computes z = ∑(wi × xi) + b, where b is a trainable bias term shifting the firing threshold.
  • Activation Function (ReLU): f(z) = max(0, z). Without non-linear activations, stacks of layers collapse into a single trivial linear equation, unable to model non-linear boundaries.
LESSON 04 - DEEP LEARNING

Hierarchical Abstraction in Deep Networks

A network is considered Deep when it contains two or more hidden layers. Depth enables hierarchical representation learning:

  • Initial Hidden Layers: Detect low-level primitive features such as edge angles and boundaries.
  • Intermediate Layers: Combine primitives into geometric contours, shapes, and textures.
  • Deep Hidden Layers: Synthesize high-level semantic concepts before reaching the final decision layer.
LESSON 05 - TRAINING & GRADIENT DESCENT

Loss Functions and Backpropagation

Neural networks do not guess; they optimize parameters iteratively through calculus:

  • Loss Function (ℒ): Measures output prediction error compared to true training labels.
  • Backpropagation: Computes analytical partial derivatives ∂ℒ/∂w for every weight via the calculus chain rule.
  • Gradient Descent: Updates weights opposite the error gradient: w ← w - η ∂ℒ/∂w, where η is the learning rate.
